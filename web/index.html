How it works

  1. Plan. A small language model rewrites a follow-up so it stands alone, and splits a broad question into up to four searches. The searches it chose appear in the trail.
  2. Search. Each search runs two ways: by meaning (an embedding model on Google Cloud Run) and by keyword (BM25). The two rankings are combined, and the first 60 passages, at most eight from any one document, go to the filter.
  3. Filter. A small language model reads those 60 passages and rates each from 0 to 3 for how much an answer to your question could use it. The 20 rated highest become the evidence. The trail lists all 60 with their ratings, so you can see what was left out. The filter only chooses passages; it never changes their text. If it fails, the search's top 20 are used and the trail says so.
  4. Answer. The model sees only those 20 passages. Each claim it makes must quote a passage word for word.
  5. Check. Code, not a model, checks every quote against the passage it cites, and that every claim in the prose is backed. A failed check goes back to the model once; an answer that still fails is shown with a warning.

What the checks guarantee, and what they don't

The checks guarantee that every quote is real and substantial. They don't guarantee that a claim follows from its quote: read the highlighted words to judge that. Figures come from reports published between 2014 and 2026; the year is shown on every passage.

When no language model is available

The service runs in evidence-only mode: it returns the most relevant passages, with their sources, and no generated answer.

The documents and passages shown here are from IP Australia's public reports, as published at ipaustralia.gov.au. The published reports are the authoritative source.
